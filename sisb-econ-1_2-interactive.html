THE PROBLEM IN THE WORLD

Empty fields in the “rice bowl” of Thailand

Thailand has the land. Thailand has the tractors. So why do rice farmers in Suphan Buri and Nakhon Sawan leave part of their fields unplanted every year?

Ask them and you hear the same answer: they cannot find anyone to do the work. Thailand’s population is ageing quickly, families are having far fewer children, and young people who finish school head to Bangkok for air-conditioned office jobs — not to the paddy fields. Farms, building sites and seafood factories now depend heavily on migrant workers from Myanmar and Cambodia. When those workers go home, the work stops.

A field with nobody to work it produces nothing. Money cannot plant rice.

The farmer owns the land and owns a tractor. Which resource is missing?

So here is the big idea. To produce anything — a bowl of som tum, a Skytrain journey, an Economics lesson — you need resources. Economists call them the factors of production and sort them into four groups: Capital, Enterprise, Land, Labour. CELL. Remove one and production stops, no matter how rich you are.

The two that catch everyone out.
The mortar and papaya shredder — “it’s made of stone and wood, so it’s land!” No. The test is not what it is made of, it is whether a human made it to help produce something else. Rubber trees in the ground are land; a rubber tyre in a factory is capital.
The street-food seller — she is two factors at once. Chopping papaya, she is labour. Choosing what to sell, setting the price and risking her own money, she is enterprise. In a small Thai business one person is often two factors; in CP All those jobs belong to thousands of different people.

The Thai story behind the table. Thailand’s working-age population is shrinking, so simply adding more workers is getting harder every year. That leaves one route: raise the quality of the labour it already has — education, training, healthcare, English and digital skills. Remember this; it comes back in Unit 4 when we ask why some economies grow faster than others.

Model answer

Write your own answer in your book first. Only then reveal this.

Explain how an increase in the number of migrant workers coming to Thailand affects the quantity of labour. [4]

MODEL ANSWER — SENTENCE STARTERS HIGHLIGHTED

Labour means the human resource used in the production process — the people who do the work.

For example, many farms in Suphan Buri and building sites in Bangkok employ migrant workers from Myanmar and Cambodia.

This happens because there are not enough Thai workers willing to do this work, so when more migrant workers arrive the number of people available to work in Thailand increases.

Therefore, the quantity of labour rises, which means farms and firms can produce more output than they could before.

Why this scores well: Define → Example → Explain → Develop, in that order. The example is specifically Thai, not “a country”. The word because is what unlocks the explanation mark, and the last sentence lands on an economic consequence instead of trailing off.
