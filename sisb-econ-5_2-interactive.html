Puzzle 1 · Thailand, 2024

About 100 baht a day

The Thai government says a person is poor if they have less than 3,078 baht a month to live on: about 100 baht a day for food, housing, transport and everything else. In 2024, 3.43 million Thais (4.89%) were below this line. In Mae Hong Son it was 25.69%.

Puzzle 2 · Bangkok, 2026

Not poor… or poor?

Khun Lek cleans offices in Bangkok for the minimum wage of 400 baht a day. She has two children. The family has food and a room to rent, so they are just above the poverty line. But they have no internet at home, and her son could not go on the Grade 6 school trip that almost all of his class went on.

So what do the puzzles show?

Share of people below the poverty line, 2024 (%). Source: NESDC, Poverty and Inequality Report 2024.

Puzzle 1: people below the poverty line cannot pay for their basic needs: food, clothing, shelter, clean water and healthcare. This is absolute poverty. It is much more common in some provinces than others.

Puzzle 2: Lek’s family can meet basic needs, but they are poor compared with other people in their country and cannot take part in normal activities. This is relative poverty.

Absolute or relative?

Absolute poverty: income is too low to meet basic needs. Relative poverty: poor compared with others in the same country, so people cannot enjoy the average standard of living. Decide what each case shows, then read the explanation.
