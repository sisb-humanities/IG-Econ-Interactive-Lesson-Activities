Think first: two Thai puzzles

Both puzzles use real Thai figures from 2025–2026. Answer the two questions under each one.

Puzzle 1 · April 2025 – March 2026

Twelve months of falling prices

For 12 months in a row, Thai prices were lower than a year earlier. In February 2026 they were 0.88% lower. In March 2026 they were 0.08% lower. The main reasons were cheaper fuel and electricity.

Puzzle 2 · October 2026

From falling prices to 3.9% in seven months

A war in the Middle East began on 28 February 2026. Thai diesel rose from 29.94 to a record 50.54 baht a litre in five weeks. By October, prices were 3.90% higher than a year earlier. Fuel prices were up 29.18%, but prices without fresh food and energy (core prices) were up only 1.58%.

So what do the puzzles show?

Thai inflation rate (% change in prices on a year earlier)Bank of Thailand target: 1–3%

Puzzle 1: a negative rate means the price level fell: that is deflation. When the rate moved from −0.88% to −0.08%, prices were still falling, just more slowly.

Puzzle 2: fuel rose far more than other prices. Fuel is a cost of production for almost every firm, so this looks like cost-push inflation, not people spending too much.

Why is it useful to know about this?

Your moneyAt 3.9% inflation, 100 baht in October 2026 bought only what about 96 baht bought a year before.
Your familyShould you save, borrow or ask for a pay rise? The answer depends on inflation.
The newsFuel prices, the Bank of Thailand’s interest rate and government handouts are all about inflation.
Your examCPI calculations, the causes of inflation and policies to control it come up often in Papers 1 and 2.

CauseWhat starts it?Clue words
