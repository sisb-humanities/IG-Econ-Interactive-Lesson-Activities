THE PROBLEM IN THE WORLD

The year Thailand lost rice and cars in the same week

October 2011. The Chao Phraya bursts its banks. Two metres of water sits inside the Rojana Industrial Estate in Ayutthaya for six weeks. Honda's plant is submerged — a thousand brand-new cars are written off. Hard-disk factories shut, and the price of computer hard drives rises around the world.

At the same moment, 1.6 million rai of paddy land is also underwater. Rice gone. Cars gone. Same flood, same weeks.

Thailand did not choose to produce less. It lost resources. That is a different thing — and economists draw it differently.

In a normal year, Thailand cannot grow more rice and build more cars at the same time without something changing. Why not?

That is the whole of this topic. A country's resources are limited, so every extra car has a price paid in rice — an opportunity cost. The production possibility curve (PPC) is simply a picture of that trade-off: the maximum combinations of two goods a country can produce when every resource is used fully and efficiently. The classic trap. Taking workers off the rice fields to build cars does not move the curve. Thailand has exactly the same resources — it has just pointed them somewhere else. The curve itself only moves when the quantity or the quality of resources changes.

Analyse the effects on Thailand of moving resources out of rice farming and into electronics factories. [6]

GRADE C — 6 MARKS — DEFINE → CONTEXT → CHAIN × 2 → OVERALL

A movement along the PPC means producing more of one good and less of another using the same total resources. In Thailand, workers and land are moving out of rice farming in Isaan and into electronics factories in the Eastern Economic Corridor.

One effect is that output of electronics rises. This leads to higher export earnings, because an electronic component sells for far more per kilogram than rice does, as a result incomes and tax revenue in Rayong increase.

Another effect is that rice output falls. This decreases the supply of rice because fewer rai are being planted, as a result the price of rice in Thai markets may rise and farming families in the north-east may lose their income.

Overall, Thailand gains manufactured output, but the opportunity cost is the rice and the rural jobs it gives up.

Why this scores well: two separate chains, each with at least two links, each anchored in a real Thai place. The word because appears in both chains. The last sentence names the opportunity cost — which is what a 6-mark PPC question is really testing.
