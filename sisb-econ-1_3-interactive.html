Unit 1.3 — Opportunity Cost

Grade 9  •  The next best alternative forgone  •  Thai context
THE PROBLEM IN THE WORLD

Eighty-two billion baht, spent once

You already ride the MRT Purple Line through Nonthaburi. Right now Thailand is spending about ฿82 billion extending it south from Tao Poon to Rat Burana — 23.6 km, 17 new stations, opening around 2030.

That money is real and it is finite. The same ฿82 billion could have re-equipped provincial hospitals across Isaan. It could have put a tablet and an internet connection in the hands of every state-school student. It could have gone into the Land Bridge ports at Ranong and Chumphon. The Bureau of the Budget cannot fund all four. It funds one.

The true cost of the Purple Line is not ฿82 billion. It is the hospital that never got built.

Think first

No definitions yet — just your brain.

The government builds the Purple Line extension. What has Thailand actually lost?

So here is the big idea. Resources are scarce — there is never enough to satisfy every want — so every economic agent has to choose. The moment you choose, you close a door. The value of the door you closed is the opportunity cost: the cost of the next best alternative forgone. Not the money you handed over. Not a list of everything you could have done. One thing — the next best one.

The two that catch everyone out.
Money cost is not opportunity cost. The ฿82 billion paid to the contractors is what Thailand spent. The hospitals it could have bought are what Thailand gave up. In the exam, an answer that says “the opportunity cost is ฿82 billion” scores zero for the definition mark.
Sunk costs are already gone. Ploy paid ฿18,000 for a console she never uses. That money is spent whatever she decides now, so it cannot be the cost of keeping it. What she gives up by keeping it is the ฿12,000 she could get by selling it today — that is the opportunity cost.

The method, every time. Find how much of one good is given up, find how much of the other is gained, then divide the first by the second. Marks are awarded for the working as well as the number — and an answer with no units (“2”) throws away a mark that “2 sacks of rice” would have earned.

Exam practice — multiple choice

Build a 4-mark answer

Put the four steps of a 4-mark answer in the right order. Tap them in sequence.

Model answer

Write your own answer in your book first. Only then reveal this.

Explain, using an example, why workers face an opportunity cost when making decisions. [4]

MODEL ANSWER — SENTENCE STARTERS HIGHLIGHTED

Opportunity cost means the cost of the next best alternative forgone when a decision is made.

For example, a worker in Thailand who trains for six years to become a doctor at Siriraj Hospital gives up the hotel management course in Phuket that was her next best option.

This happens because a worker’s time and training are scarce, so specialising in one profession means the other careers can no longer be pursued.

Therefore, the opportunity cost is the salary and career she would have had in hotel management — not the tuition fees she paid for medical school.

Why this scores well: Define → Example → Explain → Develop, in that order. The example is specifically Thai and names a real hospital, not “a worker somewhere”. The word because is what unlocks the explanation mark. Crucially, the last sentence names one thing forgone and explicitly rejects the money that was paid out — that is the development mark.

Take it home

[8] Discuss whether the Thai government was right to spend ฿82 billion on extending the MRT Purple Line rather than on upgrading provincial hospitals.

Structure: Paragraph 1 FOR (“One advantage of the Purple Line extension is…”) → Paragraph 2 AGAINST (“However, this is not always beneficial because…”) → Paragraph 3 JUDGMENT (“Overall, transport spending tends to be the better choice when…”). Most of the marks live in paragraph 3, so give a condition — when does it depend, and on what? — not a summary.
