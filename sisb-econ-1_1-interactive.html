Activity 1 · Lesson 2

Sort each item: is it a need, or a want?

A need is essential for survival — protected under Articles 25–26 of the UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights. A want is desired but not essential for survival, however normal or expensive it feels. Drag each card into the correct bin, then press Check answers.

Tip: a good test is "would this person die or suffer serious harm without it?" — not "does my family have one?"

Activity 2 · Lesson 2

Sort each example: economic good, or free good?

An economic good is limited in supply relative to demand, so it has an opportunity cost. A free good is unlimited in supply, so obtaining it costs nothing in resources. Drag each card into the correct bin.

Tip: tap water feels "free" because you don't queue for it — but treating and delivering it to your home has a real cost. That makes it an economic good.

Activity 3 · Lesson 1

Who is making the decision — consumer, firm, or government?

Economic agents are the decision-makers in an economy: consumers/households (private individuals), firms (private-sector businesses), and government (the public sector). Sort each Bangkok example into the correct bin.

Activity 4 · Reflection

Does having more money remove the basic economic problem?

Read each statement. Decide whether you think it's true, false, or partly true — then reveal the economist's view and compare your reasoning. There's no trick here: the goal is to test the idea that wealth removes scarcity.

✓ You've judged all four statements. Now try writing your own one-sentence definition of the basic economic problem that would work for a billionaire and a street vendor.

The basic economic problem never fully disappears

Wealth changes which choices are available and how painful they feel — but it never removes the need to choose. Time, truly unique goods, and even a family's attention are still limited relative to what people want, at every income level. That is exactly why the basic economic problem is described as universal, not just a problem for low-income households.
