Puzzle 1 · Q2 2026

Jobless people, empty jobs

About 401,000 Thais were unemployed. At the same time, the government’s Thai Mee Ngan Tham job website had more than 300,000 vacancies. University graduates had the highest unemployment rate of any group: 1.6%.

Puzzle 2 · Q2 2026

One of the lowest rates in the world

Thailand’s unemployment rate was only about 1%. But about 2.2 million people worked fewer hours than they wanted, and more than half of all workers are informal, with no social security.

So what do the puzzles show?

Puzzle 1: the jobs and the jobless do not match. Some people are between jobs and still searching (frictional). Others do not have the skills the vacancies need, or live in the wrong place (structural).

Puzzle 2: the rate hides a lot. The unemployment rate is a share of the labour force (employed + unemployed), and anyone who did one hour of paid work counts as employed. The 2.2 million underemployed are not in the figure.

The economy: unemployment on a PPC

At A all resources are used. When unemployment rises, the economy moves to B, inside the curve: output is lower than it could be. That lost output is the opportunity cost of unemployment.

The PPC does not move. The workers still exist, so productive capacity is the same. Only if people stay unemployed for years and lose their skills could the PPC shift inwards.
