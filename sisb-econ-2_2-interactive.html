Think first: two Thai stories

In both stories people bought more. Are they the same kind of change? Answer the two questions under each story.

Story 1 · Nonthaburi

The ฿20 Purple Line

Before October 2023 a long Purple Line trip cost up to ฿42. From 16 October 2023 fares were capped at ฿20. In the first ten months, trips on the Purple and Red Lines rose by about 26% (21.3m → 26.9m).

The cap ended on 30 September 2025, and fares went back to ฿14–42.

Story 2 · Buriram

Lisa’s favourite meatballs

In September 2021 Lisa of BLACKPINK said on Thai TV that she missed the luk chin sold outside Buriram station. Vendors’ sales jumped from a few hundred baht a day to over ฿10,000 a day.

A skewer still cost ฿5–10.

So what is the difference?

Story 1: movement along the curve. The train’s own price fell, so passengers moved down the same demand curve. Economists call this an extension in demand.

Story 2: the curve shifts. The price did not change. A non-price factor (tastes and fashion) changed, so more is wanted at every price. This is an increase in demand.
