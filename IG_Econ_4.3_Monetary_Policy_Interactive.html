For teachers — how to run this
  • Recession and Overheating first. These behave the way the textbook says, so students build confidence that the chain works. Three minutes each. Ask them to write down which aims went green and which went amber.
  • Then Thailand 2026. This one cannot be solved. Let them try every setting before you say so. The discovery — that cutting barely moves inflation, and that households will not borrow anyway — is limitations 2, 4 and 6 from Task 10, found rather than told.
  • Insist on the lag. Students will change the rate and expect an instant result. Make them run at least three quarters before judging. The growth line makes the delay visible: the turn does not happen in the quarter they decide.
  • Read the growth chart together. Orange points mark the quarters in which the rate was changed. The dashed line is potential growth — what the economy could sustain. Below it means spare capacity and rising unemployment.
  • The growth curve has a peak. Cutting raises growth — up to a point. Past it, inflation gets high enough that uncertainty drags output back down, and students end up with rising prices and falling output. Let them cut to 0% and discover stagflation for themselves, then ask them to find the top of the curve.
  • Use the cost panel to reach fiscal policy. When unemployment climbs, tax revenue falls and welfare spending rises on their own. Ask: "Has the government raised any taxes? Then why has the deficit grown?" That is the automatic stabiliser from Unit 4.2, arrived at from the other direction.
  • The question to land: "You could not fix Thailand 2026 with the interest rate. So what would you use?" Expected: targeted lending schemes for SMEs, debt restructuring, energy subsidies, supply-side policy to raise productivity — fiscal and targeted measures, not monetary policy.
