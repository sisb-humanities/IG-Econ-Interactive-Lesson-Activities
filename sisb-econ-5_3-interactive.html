Puzzle 1 · Thailand, 2025

More deaths than births

In 2025, 416,574 babies were born in Thailand: the fewest for 75 years. In the same year, 559,684 people died. From 1963 to 1983, Thailand had over one million births every year. Today a Thai woman has, on average, only about one child.

Puzzle 2 · Samut Sakhon, 2026

Who will do the work?

Thailand has more than 3.6 million registered migrant workers, most of them from Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos. Many work in factories, construction, fishing and farming. When thousands of Cambodian workers went home during the 2025 border conflict, firms struggled to fill the jobs, and the government began recruiting workers from Sri Lanka.

So what do the puzzles show?

Births in Thailand (thousands). The orange line shows deaths in 2025. Sources: Mahidol University IPSR; Ministry of Public Health; Department of Provincial Administration.

Puzzle 1: a population grows when births are higher than deaths (a natural increase). In Thailand, deaths are now higher than births, so without migration the population falls. It has fallen this way every year since 2021.

Puzzle 2: population also changes through migration. More people moving in (immigration) than moving out (emigration) raises the population and adds to the labour force.

Why is it useful to know about this?

Your countryThailand is shrinking and getting older faster than almost any other middle-income country. One Thai in five is now aged 60 or over.
Government choicesBaby bonuses, a later retirement age, work permits for migrants: these are in the news every month. Which ones work?
Your futureYour generation will be a smaller workforce supporting more older people. That affects your taxes, your job and your own retirement.
Your examBirth rate, net migration, optimum population, the dependency ratio and “Discuss” questions on ageing come up often.

Leaving Myanmar

Conflict since the 2021 coup has pushed many more young people to leave Myanmar. Over two million Myanmar workers are registered in Thailand. Most are of working age, and they include skilled workers such as nurses and engineers.

Leaving Thailand

About 145,000 Thais work legally abroad, mainly in Taiwan, South Korea, Japan and Israel. Thai workers abroad sent home about 270 billion baht in the 2024 fiscal year.
